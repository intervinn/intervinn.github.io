first post after hello, im gonna rant about ai the day before exam.

currently ai is everywhere - it's in the sites, the codebases and our normal life. i use ai myself, i constantly use it to help me with
studying and research. more developers are shifting from an absolutely negative view to a more weighted and positive opinion

everybody likes clankers, they can write us texts, code, search and summarize for us. we can roleplay and debate with them
if everyone else in our environment is too stupid or not knowledgeable enough for specific topics.

but often, very often, the human benefits don't go well with the market benefits. one thing we should mark is that most, if not all
ai-related services are free to use in the beginning. let's be fair, that is VERY generous: llms require TONS of memory and computational
resources.

but the reason why most ai services are free to use at this moment is very obvious: the companies are working on making this a consumer habit, and if they succeed while ai belongs to cloud, they'll reap huge benefits from it. now that ai is clearly demanded by people, you can see how they try to commercialize it now: adding advertisements into responses and reducing the free prompts amount

while experts speak of revolutional changes and huge money, the only thing that i see is that only nvidia has actually profited, while oracle is losing millions over it's too confident investments.

this was a general prelude, two things i wanted to tell about is
how vibecoding is useless in incompetent and unknowledgeable hands, and how ai as a technology seems too far from maturing

first off, i often like to scroll the project showcases in numerous discord server, aswell as twitter. pretty often it's the simple newbie stuff, or something more interesting. sometimes it's really cool stuff with very brave goals: programming languages, game libraries, web frameworks, do you know what easily identifies that their author likely has no idea what they're making? pointless and undetailed project description, weird documentation and messy project structure, and seeing claude, AGENTS.md or .gemini/.claude directories connect the puzzle. they likely wrote something like "hey claude, let's flip the world and make a programming language as fast as go, but as readable as python, here's the code snippet". well, claude did it, but it did what it did: a programming language which is probably as fast as go and and as simple as python, a game library which might run across all platforms using just javascript, a web framework that is the fastest of them all. 

now these projects catch attention and stars, but they are completely soulless. the vibecoder behind the project likely isn't aware of how to make his idea work, which techniques and algorithms essential make the framework work faster, his project doesn't have a clear philosophy or deep technical details to let the others learn. ai just did it's job, wrote some messy implementation, tried to connect something that doesn't connect.

that is why i think ai isn't great of an innovator, and that is by design - at their foundation they're the summarization and text analysis clankers. they'll read something and tell why that software used that, but it won't ever make something from scratch.

so, dear innovators, dear beginners, don't try to embrace ai the same as seniors embrace them. in the skilled hands even a dick can be a balalaika, they can write things without ai, while we, as beginners, can't if we just keep asking grok to write us something. that is what i think.

finally, ai, as a technology, seems very immature. right now it belongs to clouds and companies, and it's not too far from being
a commercialized privilege. artificial intelligence doesn't belong humanity yet. it belongs to companies. i believe that when ai matures out is when it gets standardized and the smart models become open sourced, aswell as when the models themselves get researched better and become more efficient (and i believe google does things right, they make hardware more efficient, aswell as make the models more compact)

so some day openai might actually be open and publish some open initiatives and standards, but well.. with it's awful leadership it definitely won't
